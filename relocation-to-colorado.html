Choosing an area

Not sure where in Colorado you want to live?

That is the normal starting point, and it is the right question to work through before looking at houses.

Colorado is a big state, and communities here differ enormously from one another. Two neighborhoods thirty minutes apart can offer completely different lifestyles, commute patterns, housing stock, lot sizes, and price points. A place that suits someone commuting downtown daily may be the wrong choice for someone working remotely or heading to the airport every week.

Rather than tell you where to live, I will help you compare real areas against the things that actually matter to you:

Commute Home prices Property types New construction Neighborhood character Outdoor access Schools Amenities Work location

On schools in particular, I will point you to the official district and state sources and let you evaluate them yourself, rather than offering opinions about which are better. The same goes for taxes and anything else where you deserve primary sources rather than a broker's characterization.

Start here

Your move doesn't have to start with a house.

Most people begin by scrolling listings, which is the natural instinct and usually the least useful first step. A house only makes sense once you know which part of Colorado you want to be in and what you actually need from a home.

So we start there instead. Where do you need to be during the week, what does your day look like, how much space do you really use, and what would make this move feel like an upgrade rather than a compromise. Once that is clear, the search gets dramatically simpler, and you stop wasting time on homes that were never going to work.

There is no cost for this, and no obligation. Plenty of the people I talk to are six to twelve months out and just want to understand the landscape.
